Living in Medina del Campo

A royal market town with a great brick castle, one of Spain's largest main squares and trains to Madrid in under an hour, from about €1,100 per m².

Why Medina del Campo stands out

  • Madrid Chamartín is about 55 minutes away by direct train, with around 12 trains a day; Valladolid is 25–35 minutes, with about 28.
  • Flats cost about €1,137 per m², less than half the Spanish average, and a 2-bedroom flat typically sells for €72,000–108,000.
  • It has its own hospital with A&E and the main specialities, serving more than 53,000 people.
  • The Plaza Mayor, built for the great fairs of the 15th and 16th centuries, is still full of terraces and tapas bars.
  • Holy Week is of International Tourist Interest, and the August Renaissance fair brings thousands of people in period dress.
  • The Rueda white-wine vineyards start at the edge of town, and six provincial capitals are within 100 km.

Homes and prices in Medina del Campo

Flats in Medina del Campo cost about €1,137 per m² and houses about €1,001 per m² (October 2026, RealAdvisor). A 75 m² flat therefore works out at around €85,000. That is 59% below the Spanish average for flats, €2,787 per m².

These are averages. Homes that need work, or that have been on the market for a while, often sell well below them, and that is where our negotiation makes the difference.

A short history

Medina del Campo grew up in the 11th century on the mota, the low hill where the castle stands today. From the 15th century its two great fairs a year drew merchants and bankers from all over Europe, and much of the trade and credit of the time was agreed under the arcades of its main square. Ferdinand and Isabella used the town as one of their favourite residences, and Queen Isabella I signed her will and died here in 1504. Today it is a working town of about 20,000 people that lives from trade, farming and furniture making, which is why it is also called the City of Furniture.

Things to see and do in Medina del Campo

  • Castillo de la MotaA huge red-brick fortress commissioned in the mid-15th century by Juan II and Enrique IV, later a royal archive and a state prison. The guided visit (about 1 hour, €5) takes you along the walls, the underground firing gallery, the parade ground and the chapel; the 40 m keep is a separate 50-minute visit (€5, or €9 for both). Some weekends there are theatre-style tours (€8, booking needed).
  • Plaza Mayor de la HispanidadBuilt for the fairs, it is one of the largest main squares in Spain, about one and a half hectares. Around it stand the Collegiate Church of San Antolín, the town hall, the Casa de los Arcos and the 17th-century Casa del Peso, which is the tourist office (Plaza Mayor 48).
  • Collegiate Church of San AntolínFounded in 1177 and rebuilt over the centuries. Look for the Balcón del Pópulo, an outdoor balcony from which Mass was said for the crowds at the fair, and go up the tower on a guided visit.
  • Palacio Real TestamentarioThe royal palace on the main square where Isabella I signed her will and died in 1504, now a museum about her life and reign.
  • Museo de las FeriasA museum unique in Spain about the fairs that made Medina one of the trading centres of Europe in the 15th and 16th centuries: coins, scales, account books and letters of exchange.
  • Reales CarniceríasThe old royal meat market, a long arcaded building used today for exhibitions and as a stage during the Renaissance Week.
  • Saint Teresa and Saint John of the CrossSaint Teresa of Ávila founded her second convent here in 1567, and it was in Medina that she met the young Saint John of the Cross. Near the main square stands the chapel of San Juan de la Cruz, where he is said to have said his first Mass.
  • Palacio de las Salinas spaFour kilometres out of town, a palace-style spa hotel built in 1912 by the team behind the Magdalena Palace in Santander. Its mineral waters were declared of public benefit in 1893. It is the only spa in the province and is open to non-residents.
  • Sunday marketEvery Sunday from 9:00 to 14:00, about 200 stalls fill calle José Zorrilla: fruit, clothes, cooked food and household goods. It is one of the largest weekly markets in Castile and León. Go early: after 12:00 the aisles get crowded.

The main square and eating out

The Plaza Mayor is where Medina lives. People meet for a vino before lunch and again in the evening, and the square fills with terraces in good weather. Every bar is known for something: mussels at Alegría, prawns and tigres (stuffed mussels) at El Casino, prawns at Mónaco 1962. A small tip from visitors: on Thursdays many bars close, so check before you go.

  • Mónaco 1962 (Plaza Mayor)Home cooking and one of the best-rated places in town; a weekday set menu of about €17 with four or five choices per course.
  • Bar Casino (Plaza Mayor)Popular for its low prices, prawns and stuffed mussels.
  • Alegría (Plaza Mayor)Sharing plates and mussels on a terrace facing the square.
  • Gloria (Plaza Mayor)Castilian dishes with some modern touches, in two dining rooms.
  • Tapería La PlazaCreative tapas based on seasonal and Mediterranean produce.
  • Mesón La PlazaIn the cellar of the Casa del Peso, open for more than 50 years.
  • El MorteroToasts with local ingredients, and roast suckling pig.
  • Taberna MohínoLarge and busy; known for its cocido (chickpea stew) on the set menu.

Restaurants and prices at the time of writing; opening days change, so check before you go.

The year in Medina del Campo

Every SundayBig street market on calle José Zorrilla, 9:00–14:00.
March or AprilHoly Week, declared of International Tourist Interest. Its processions of discipline go back to 1411 and are considered the oldest in Spain; 17 processions, and the Miserere sung in the Plaza Mayor. In 2026 it ran from 27 March to 5 April.
Early MayD.O. Rueda Wine Fair in the Plaza Mayor, with more than 20 wineries, tastings and local food, followed by the Spring Fair (8–10 May in 2026).
AugustRenaissance Week and the Imperiales y Comuneros Fair: a 16th-century market with around 200 stalls, thousands of people in period dress, camps, concerts and the re-enactment of the burning of Medina in 1520. In 2026: 18–21 August.
SeptemberFerias y Fiestas de San Antolín, the town's main festival, with traditional bull runs declared of Regional Tourist Interest.

Food and drink

The classic meal is lechazo, milk-fed lamb roasted in a wood oven, with a white wine from the D.O. Rueda, whose vineyards start at the edge of town. Also look for Castilian cocido, garlic soup and game, and for dessert the local cocadas, small coconut sweets.

Nature and outdoors

The land around Medina is a flat plain of cereal fields, pine woods and vineyards, crossed by the river Zapardiel. It is easy cycling country: a 32 km loop runs through the villages of Moraleja de las Panaderas and Gomeznarro, and an 83 km 'Route of the Castles' links Medina, Íscar and Olmedo. For a river landscape, the Riberas de Castronuño–Vega del Duero nature reserve is a short drive north.

Everyday life and services

Hospital de Medina del Campo (calle Peñaranda 24) serves more than 53,000 people in the south of the province, with A&E, paediatrics, obstetrics, cardiology, trauma, ophthalmology and other specialities, and works with the Hospital Clínico Universitario in Valladolid. There are two health centres (Medina urbano and Medina rural). Sport: the Pablo Cáceres sports centre and the municipal stadium. The population was 20,314 at the 2021 census; about 29% are over 60.

Getting around: trains, buses and roads

Train to MadridAbout 12 direct trains a day (AVE, Alvia, Avlo and regional), from roughly 55 minutes to Madrid Chamartín; tickets from about €12. Slower regional trains go to Príncipe Pío in about 2 h 20.
Train to ValladolidAround 28 trains a day, 25–35 minutes, from about €4. Easy for a daily commute.
Train to SalamancaAbout 7 direct trains a day, 40–45 minutes, from about €5.
StationsThe classic station is about 700 m from the centre; high-speed trains also stop at Medina del Campo AV.
BusAbout 8 buses a day to Valladolid (La Regional, about 40 minutes, from €5; first around 7:00, last around 21:15). To Madrid, 4–8 a day with Monbus and others (about 2 hours, from €12), from calle Ronda Gracia Plazuela. Buses also go to Lugo and Galicia (Alsa).
RoadOn the A-6 Madrid–A Coruña motorway, exits 156, 157, 160 and 161. Madrid is about 140–160 km away: the A-6 is toll-free from Medina to Adanero, then the AP-6 toll section crosses the Guadarrama mountains. Valladolid is 47 km by the old road or 53 km by motorway via Tordesillas (35–40 minutes); the A-62 to Salamanca and Burgos starts 25 km north at Tordesillas.
AirportsValladolid–Villanubla is about 49 km away and Salamanca–Matacán about 60 km. For most international flights, Madrid-Barajas is reached by train via Chamartín.
Six capitals within 100 kmValladolid, Salamanca, Zamora, Ávila, Segovia and Palencia are all under 100 km away.

The property market in Medina del Campo

In October 2026 the average asking price in Medina del Campo was €1,102 per m² (flats €1,137, houses €1,001), up 12.2% in a year and 28% in three years (RealAdvisor). Another portal, Indomio, gave €898 per m² in July 2026, up 11.3%. Typical prices by size: a studio from about €67,000, a 2-bedroom flat €72,000–108,000, a 3-bedroom flat €90,000–135,000 and a 4-bedroom flat €120,000–180,000. The villages within the municipality are cheaper (around €878 per m² for flats in postcode 47492). Rents: a median of about €784 a month for a flat on RealAdvisor, while Indomio puts asking rents at €3.90 per m² a month; gross yields therefore range from roughly 5% to 8% depending on the source and the property.

Who Medina del Campo suits

Remote workers and commuters who need fast trains to Madrid or Valladolid, retirees who want a hospital and a lively square within walking distance, families on a budget, and investors looking for rental yields of roughly 5–8%.

The honest downsides

  • A continental climate: cold, foggy winters and hot, dry summers. You will want good heating.
  • Midweek the centre can be quiet, and many bars close on Thursdays.
  • Prices have risen fast (about 12% in a year), so negotiation matters more than it did.
  • Outside the tourist sites, expect to need some Spanish for daily life.

Day trips from Medina del Campo

  • Rueda (about 12 km)The capital of Rueda white wine, with wineries to visit and a fine baroque church.
  • Tordesillas (about 25 km)Where the Treaty of Tordesillas was signed in 1494; Royal Monastery of Santa Clara and a medieval bridge over the Duero.
  • Olmedo (about 25 km)Mudéjar churches, a thermal spa and a park of scale models of Mudéjar monuments, good with children.
  • Coca (about 40 km)Coca Castle, often called the most impressive Mudéjar building in Spain.
  • Valladolid (about 50 km)A city of 300,000 with shopping, culture and the regional university hospital, 25 minutes by train.
  • Salamanca (about 80 km)One of Spain's most beautiful cities, 40 minutes by train.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the train from Medina del Campo to Madrid?

The fastest trains take about 55 minutes to Madrid Chamartín, with around 12 direct trains a day; tickets start at about €12.

Is there a hospital in Medina del Campo?

Yes. The Hospital de Medina del Campo has A&E and the main specialities and serves more than 53,000 people; the university hospital in Valladolid is 25 minutes away by train.

Can I live in Medina del Campo without a car?

In town, yes: the centre, the station, the hospital and the shops are close together, and there are frequent trains to Valladolid, Madrid and Salamanca. A car helps for the villages and wineries around.

What is there to do in the evening?

Life centres on the Plaza Mayor: terraces, tapas bars and restaurants, with special evenings during Holy Week, the May wine fair, the Renaissance Week in August and the San Antolín fiestas in September.

What does a flat cost in Medina del Campo?

Flats in Medina del Campo cost about €1,137 per m² and houses about €1,001 per m² (October 2026, RealAdvisor). A 75 m² flat therefore works out at around €85,000. That is 59% below the Spanish average for flats, €2,787 per m².

What rental return can I expect?

Gross yields range roughly from 5% to 8% depending on the property and the source, with a median flat rent of about €784 a month on RealAdvisor (October 2026).
