Buying a home in Spain: costs and taxes for foreigners

When you buy a resale home in Castile and León you pay a transfer tax of 8% (10% on the part of the price above €250,000), plus notary and land registry fees. After that, owners pay a yearly local tax, and non-residents also file a yearly income tax return.

By La Comercial Inmobiliaria · Legal and tax figures checked November 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer: budget the price of the home plus roughly 10% for taxes and fees when you buy. In Castile and León, where Villablino is, most of that is the transfer tax (ITP) of 8% (10% on the part of the price above €250,000).

1. Taxes and costs when you buy

Transfer tax (ITP) on resale homes

Almost every home we show is a resale home, so you pay the Impuesto de Transmisiones Patrimoniales (ITP). The rate is set by each region. In Castile and León it is 8% (10% on the part of the price above €250,000).

There are lower rates for some people buying their main home: 4% for buyers under 36, large families, people with a disability of 65% or more and buyers of protected housing, with income limits. In small rural towns the rate can drop to 0.01% for buyers under 36 buying their first main home for under €150,000 in a municipality of up to 10,000 people (income limits apply). Villablino has 7,585 residents (INE, 2025), so it counts as a rural town for this rule, but you must meet every condition.

New homes

New homes bought from a developer pay VAT instead of ITP, plus stamp duty (AJD) on the deed. Ask us for the figures if you are looking at a new build.

2. Taxes while you own the home

  • IBI: a yearly municipal tax on the cadastral value; the rate depends on the town hall. In small towns it is usually modest. We ask the seller for the last receipt so you know the figure.
  • Non-resident income tax: if you do not live in Spain, you pay a yearly tax on the home even if you don't rent it out: 1.1% of the cadastral value (2% if it has not been revised in the last ten years), taxed at 19% for EU/EEA residents or 24% for everyone else. It is filed on form Modelo 210.
  • Rental income: if you rent the home out, non-residents pay tax on that income too (19% or 24%).

3. If you become a Spanish tax resident

If you live in Spain for more than 183 days a year, or your main interests are here, you normally become a Spanish tax resident and pay Spanish income tax on your worldwide income. Your home country's tax treaty with Spain decides how pensions and other income are taxed. Get advice from a cross-border tax adviser before you move.

4. When you sell

If you sell while you are a non-resident, the buyer withholds 3% of the price and pays it to the tax office (Modelo 211). It is an advance payment towards your capital gains tax, and you can claim back any excess. The seller also usually pays the municipal plusvalía tax.

Frequently asked questions

What is the transfer tax in Castile and León?

8% (10% on the part of the price above €250,000) for resale homes, with reduced rates for some main-home buyers.

Do non-residents pay tax on a home they don't rent out?

Yes. 1.1% of the cadastral value (2% if it has not been revised in the last ten years), taxed at 19% for EU/EEA residents or 24% for everyone else, filed on Modelo 210.

What happens when a non-resident sells?

The buyer withholds 3% of the price and pays it to the Spanish tax office as an advance on the seller's capital gains tax.
