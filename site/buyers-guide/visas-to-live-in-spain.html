Visas to live in Spain in 2026

Buying a home in Spain does not give you the right to live here. If you are not an EU citizen you need a visa, and the three most used ones are the digital nomad visa, the non-lucrative visa and the working holiday visa.

By La Comercial Inmobiliaria · Legal and tax figures checked November 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer: remote workers usually choose the digital nomad visa (an income of €2,849 a month for a single applicant in 2026, which is 200% of the Spanish minimum wage, and no more than 20% of the work for Spanish clients). Retirees and people living on savings usually choose the non-lucrative visa (€28,800 a year for the main applicant in 2026, which is 400% of the IPREM index, plus €7,200 for each family member, and you cannot work in Spain). Young Australians, New Zealanders and Canadians can use the working holiday visa (up to 12 months of living and working in Spain (ages 18–30 for Australians and New Zealanders, 18–35 for Canadians)).

Digital nomad visa (remote work)

This visa lets you live in Spain while you work remotely for companies or clients outside Spain. You need to show an income of €2,849 a month for a single applicant in 2026, which is 200% of the Spanish minimum wage, and no more than 20% of the work for Spanish clients. The amount rises every year because it is tied to the Spanish minimum wage.

Non-lucrative visa (retirees and savers)

This visa is for people who want to live in Spain without working here. You need to show €28,800 a year for the main applicant in 2026, which is 400% of the IPREM index, plus €7,200 for each family member, and you cannot work in Spain. You also need private health insurance.

Working holiday visa (under 31, or under 36 for Canadians)

Spain has youth mobility agreements that give up to 12 months of living and working in Spain (ages 18–30 for Australians and New Zealanders, 18–35 for Canadians). It is a good way to try life in Spain before buying.

UK citizens: the 90/180 rule

Since Brexit, UK passport holders can stay up to 90 days in any 180-day period in the Schengen area without a visa. Owning a home in Spain does not change this. To stay longer you need one of the visas above.

What about the golden visa?

Spain closed its investor residence route (the "golden visa") for property purchases in 2025, so buying a home no longer gives you residence by itself.

Frequently asked questions

How much income do I need for the Spanish digital nomad visa?

An income of €2,849 a month for a single applicant in 2026, which is 200% of the Spanish minimum wage, and no more than 20% of the work for Spanish clients.

How much do I need for the non-lucrative visa?

€28,800 a year for the main applicant in 2026, which is 400% of the IPREM index, plus €7,200 for each family member, and you cannot work in Spain.

Does buying a property in Spain give me residency?

No. You need a visa such as the digital nomad or non-lucrative visa if you are not an EU citizen.
