Moving to Spain from the US: the visas that actually work

Americans can stay in Spain as tourists for up to 90 days. To live here you need a visa, and the two that fit most people are the digital nomad visa (an income of €2,849 a month for a single applicant in 2026, which is 200% of the Spanish minimum wage, and no more than 20% of the work for Spanish clients) and the non-lucrative visa (€28,800 a year for the main applicant in 2026, which is 400% of the IPREM index, plus €7,200 for each family member, and you cannot work in Spain).

By La Comercial Inmobiliaria · Legal and tax figures checked November 2026 · 7 min read

Option 1: digital nomad visa

For remote employees of US companies and freelancers with clients outside Spain. You need an income of €2,849 a month for a single applicant in 2026, which is 200% of the Spanish minimum wage, and no more than 20% of the work for Spanish clients.

Option 2: non-lucrative visa

For retirees and people living on savings, investments or pensions, with no work in Spain. You need €28,800 a year for the main applicant in 2026, which is 400% of the IPREM index, plus €7,200 for each family member, and you cannot work in Spain. You also need private health insurance.

Taxes: the American exception

The US taxes its citizens on worldwide income wherever they live, and Spain taxes its residents too. The US–Spain tax treaty helps avoid paying twice, but you need a cross-border tax adviser before you move.

Frequently asked questions

Can Americans live in Spain?

Yes, with a visa such as the digital nomad visa or the non-lucrative visa.

How much income do I need for the Spanish digital nomad visa?

An income of €2,849 a month for a single applicant in 2026, which is 200% of the Spanish minimum wage, and no more than 20% of the work for Spanish clients.

Do Americans in Spain still file US taxes?

Yes. US citizens file US returns wherever they live; a tax treaty helps avoid double taxation.
